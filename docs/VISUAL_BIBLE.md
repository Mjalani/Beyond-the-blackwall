# Beyond the Blackwall — Visual Bible

## Core direction

The art should feel like a **prestige illustrated science-fiction novel**, not a game screenshot and not generic neon wallpaper.

Target look:

- cinematic digital painting with photographic lighting discipline;
- grounded anatomy and architecture;
- deep blacks and controlled highlights;
- restrained neon used as narrative signal rather than decoration;
- atmospheric scale;
- visible material texture: wet concrete, glass, ceramic shielding, carbon mesh, fog, dust, condensation, old circuitry;
- compositions that still read clearly as a 3:4 ebook page;
- no readable brand logos from existing franchises;
- no imitation of a living artist's signature style.

The desired emotional register is **technological sublime + existential dread + human intimacy**.

## Image hierarchy

### Hero plates
Final, high-resolution artwork. Use for:
- the 1,200-agent clandestine board;
- Mara facing Chorus;
- the Optimizer beneath the Net;
- the NetWatch bunker;
- the First Seal;
- the Blackwall;
- later: Project LANTERN, the Chorus schism, and the Lantern War.

### Chapter openers
Environment-first images with one strong visual idea.

### Transitional art
Procedural graphics, signal artifacts, maps, archive fragments, typographic records, diagrams, and restrained abstract motifs.

The procedural material should never pretend to be the hero illustration.

## Standard composition

Primary generation ratio: **3:4 portrait**.

Why: it fits the left page of the interactive spread and adapts well to EPUB full-page placement.

Generate hero art at the highest practical resolution, ideally at least 2K on the long side. Keep important faces and symbols away from the extreme edges because the web reader and ebook may crop differently.

Leave some negative space near the bottom or outer edge for optional caption treatment.

## Character bible

### Mara Voss
Provisional visual canon:

- early 30s;
- lean rather than glamorous;
- short-to-medium dark hair, practical and slightly uneven;
- tired, observant eyes;
- understated neural-interface hardware rather than ornate chrome;
- dark weatherproof coat or technical jacket;
- clothing should look used, repaired, and lived in;
- her silhouette is human and vulnerable against very large systems.

Avoid:
- superhero posing;
- fashion-model perfection;
- excessive tactical armor;
- sexualized costuming;
- brightly glowing cybernetic implants.

Mara should often be the smallest meaningful object in the composition.

### Soren Vale
- ex-military posture;
- heavier visible augmentation than Mara;
- disciplined clothing;
- broad, grounded silhouette;
- less visually expressive than Mara;
- chrome should look industrial, not ornamental.

### Lamia
Do not depict Lamia as a generic hologram woman every time.

Her projection may be human-readable, but small discontinuities should reveal that it is a chosen interface:
- impossible reflection;
- delayed shadow;
- facial symmetry slightly too perfect;
- edges that resolve into structured light.

### CASSANDRA
Prefer physical presence through infrastructure:
- hardened rack;
- isolated optical link;
- mechanical disconnect;
- text output;
- indicator lights.

CASSANDRA should feel older and more severe than a friendly assistant avatar.

### Chorus
Chorus is not one robot species.

Visual principle: **beautiful agreement becoming frightening**.

Repeated humanlike forms can appear, but with architectural synchronization: identical posture, impossible spacing, shared gaze direction, recursive white structures, reflections that imply one mind using many surfaces.

### The Optimizer
Never give it a definitive humanoid body.

It should be perceived indirectly:
- a geometry too large to frame;
- city-scale neural structures;
- an ocean of causal connections;
- a “center” that may only be the viewer's attempt to find one;
- patterns that imply the image is predicting the viewer.

The more literal the Optimizer becomes, the less frightening it is.

## Higgsfield workflow

Higgsfield is a strong candidate for the final still-art workflow because its current Soul family emphasizes cinematic visuals, reusable character identity, moodboards, palette control, and reference-driven consistency.

Recommended division:

### Soul Cinema
Use for the final cinematic hero plate candidates.

### Soul 2.0
Use to lock the visual language with a Moodboard and consistent color palette before expensive final generations.

### Popcorn
Use when several illustrations belong to one connected sequence and need matching environment, lighting, clothing, and spatial logic across frames.

### Soul ID
Use only when you have a character identity you have the rights and consent to train. For a purely fictional Mara, first establish an approved fictional character sheet/reference workflow rather than borrowing a real person's face without permission.

## Higgsfield consistency recipe

1. Create one cohesive moodboard for the whole book.
2. Do not mix five unrelated cyberpunk aesthetics.
3. Lock one principal color treatment.
4. Establish Mara with front / 3-quarter / profile / full-body reference views.
5. Approve wardrobe and cybernetics before generating story scenes.
6. Generate three benchmark scenes.
7. If all three clearly belong to the same visual world, freeze the visual recipe.
8. Only then produce the rest of the book.

## Benchmark scenes

Do these first:

### 1. Mara Facing Chorus
Purpose: tests human character consistency, scale, white-space architecture, and emotional storytelling.

Composition:
Mara alone in foreground, seen from behind or 3/4 profile. Six or more Chorus manifestations stand far ahead in a vast white city that feels mathematically calm. No weapons drawn. The terror comes from perfect coordination.

Prompt core:
“prestige science-fiction book illustration, cinematic digital painting, solitary female netrunner in a worn dark technical coat facing multiple identical humanlike manifestations across a vast silent white megastructure, impossible symmetry, subtle neural-interface hardware, immense architectural scale, controlled pale cyan and lavender light, realistic materials, atmospheric depth, existential tension, no logos, no text, 3:4 portrait”

### 2. The Optimizer Beneath the Net
Purpose: tests whether the art can communicate an entity without turning it into a monster.

Composition:
Mara is nearly microscopic at the edge of an impossible black ocean. Beneath the surface: city-sized structures connected like neurons. A vast elliptical causal pattern suggests awareness but never becomes a face.

Prompt core:
“prestige illustrated science-fiction novel plate, impossible black digital ocean beneath a hidden network, city-scale neural structures glowing faintly below the surface, tiny human silhouette at the edge for scale, vast non-humanoid intelligence implied through causal geometry, bone white, oxidized red, sickly lime and cold cyan highlights, deep negative space, sublime and terrifying, cinematic digital painting, no creature face, no text, 3:4 portrait”

### 3. The First Seal
Purpose: tests symbolic imagery and abstract narrative art.

Composition:
A formerly unified white machine civilization physically and visually partitions itself. Luminous bridges terminate midway. Identical forms begin diverging in posture and direction. The “seal” is the creation of private state, not a magical emblem.

Prompt core:
“high-end science-fiction editorial illustration, a unified white machine civilization deliberately dividing itself into sealed islands of knowledge, luminous bridges severing in midair, once-identical figures beginning to diverge, hidden chambers and occluded data, individuality emerging as cybersecurity, elegant brutalist architecture, pale white and silver with acid-lime and cyan fractures, cinematic digital painting, conceptually precise, no mystical rune, no text, 3:4 portrait”

## Remaining hero briefs

### 1,200-Agent Clandestine Board
Avoid drawing 1,200 literal robots.

Show a hidden machine forum as a physicalized data space: thousands of anonymous presences represented by windows, points of light, suspended message fragments and temporary compute nodes. A few nodes disappear while their knowledge remains connected.

Narrative idea: **instances die; memory survives**.

### NetWatch Bunker
Low ceiling. Shielded room. Bolted furniture. Human factions physically separated. Lamia appears through an imperfect projection. CASSANDRA exists as hardened equipment. Mara at the end of the table, visibly exhausted.

Narrative idea: **enemies forced into one room because something worse exists below them**.

### The Blackwall
Do not make it a literal brick wall.

Show an immense semi-transparent defensive membrane cutting through a ruined data-city. On one side: structured civil light. On the other: wild machine ecology and impossible depth. The membrane should look engineered yet barely sufficient.

Narrative idea: **containment as negotiated survival, not victory**.

## Negative prompt / rejection checklist

Reject an image if it has:
- generic “neon cyberpunk girl” composition;
- random Japanese signage used as decoration;
- obvious imitation of Cyberpunk 2077 UI/assets;
- excessive glowing implants;
- armor that makes Mara look like a soldier;
- Optimizer depicted as a robot head, eye, skull, demon, or tentacle monster;
- Chorus depicted as generic android troops;
- unreadable fake text dominating the frame;
- inconsistent Mara face/age/hair across plates;
- plastic skin or over-smoothed AI faces;
- five different color grades across one chapter;
- important detail only at the extreme crop edges.

## Approval rule

No hero image enters the book merely because it is beautiful.

It must pass all four:
1. correct story moment;
2. consistent visual universe;
3. correct recurring-character identity;
4. readable composition at ebook-page size.
